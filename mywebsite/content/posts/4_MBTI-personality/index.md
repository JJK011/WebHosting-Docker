# A Personality Test Helped Me Understand Myself!

The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is a self-report questionnaire that categorizes people into one of 16 personality types based on how they perceive the world and make decisions. It measures preferences across four spectrums: Introversion/Extraversion, Sensing/Intuition, Thinking/Feeling, and Judging/Perceiving.

That said, MBTI is not considered scientifically reliable. Studies have shown that many people get different results when they retake the test. It forces human personality into strict either/or categories instead of measuring traits on a spectrum. Additionally, the test was created by two authors with no formal training in psychology and was based more on theories than controlled clinical studies. Because of these issues, MBTI lacks strong predictive power for things like job performance, making it a poor tool for hiring or career counseling.

Even with all of that being true, many people still find MBTI useful as a tool for self-reflection or understanding how they interact with others.

I first came across this test last year and decided to give it a try. The result I got genuinely surprised me because there were so many similarities between the description and my own personality. I know that if I see even 50% similarity, my mind might automatically assume the remaining 50% is also true. I kept that possibility in mind while reading. Yet, despite being aware of that bias, I still found a lot of the strengths and weaknesses described to be surprisingly relatable.

So, whether it is scientifically accurate or not, and even if my type changes a few years from now, I find it useful for understanding myself today. For now, I'm choosing to use it as a tool for self-improvement rather than treating it as some absolute truth.

And with that disclaimer out of the way, let's talk about the personality type I got: **INFJ**.

## What Is an INFJ?

According to the official MBTI website:

> INFJ (Advocate) is a personality type with the Introverted, Intuitive, Feeling, and Judging traits. They tend to approach life with deep thoughtfulness and imagination. Their inner vision, personal values, and a quiet, principled version of humanism guide them in all things.
>
> Idealistic and principled, people with the INFJ personality type aren't content to coast through life – they want to stand up and make a difference. For these compassionate personalities, success doesn't come from money or status but from seeking fulfillment, helping others, and being a force for good in the world.

From an outside perspective, some people might read this and think, *"Wow, this guy really sees himself as some perfect human being."*

Trust me, that's not the case.

As someone who is highly insecure about himself, I know very well that I'm far from perfect. But when I read descriptions like this, I don't interpret them as *"this is exactly who I am."* Instead, I see them as *"this is who I would like to be."*

It's not that I don't care about money or success. Of course I do. But at the end of the day, if I'm able to do work I enjoy and be helpful to others along the way, I feel genuinely fulfilled.

## Social Mask??

One observation about INFJs that caught my attention was this:

> INFJs often have their social game perfected. They know how to act and what to say in order to be viewed positively. They understand the rules of interaction. However, they may rely on those rules so heavily that they end up hiding their true feelings because being completely authentic feels too vulnerable.

I think this is true for me.

Part of it probably comes from being a socially anxious and insecure person. Reading that might make me sound like some sort of supervillain carefully manipulating social situations, but honestly, it's much simpler than that.

Most of the time, I genuinely try to be kind because I assume people won't like me unless I make an effort. Maybe that comes from years of bullying and low self-esteem. I don't know.

What I do know is that the effort is genuine. I try to be a good person because I want to be one. And if I don't like someone, I usually don't manipulate them or pretend otherwise—I just avoid interacting with them.

## Being Too Certain...Am I??

Another description I found interesting was this:

> INFJs can become rigid in their opinions, plans, and viewpoints. They take a long time to reach conclusions, but once they do, those conclusions can feel like absolute truth. They struggle to absorb new information when they believe they've already figured things out.

Honestly, that sounds familiar too.

I like certainty. I like understanding how things work and predicting how things will unfold. When reality refuses to cooperate with my carefully crafted understanding, I can become uncomfortable.

And maybe my willingness to believe in MBTI itself is an example of that tendency. Hehe.

## Criticism and Vulnerability...Yep!

This next one hit especially hard:

> INFJs are often averse to criticism, especially when they feel that their deeply held values are being challenged.

This is definitely true for me.

Ironically, I don't enjoy praise very much either. But criticism affects me far more strongly.

Sometimes I take it personally even when it is completely valid. Looking back, many of the arguments I've had with friends, family members, or even my brother were caused by this tendency. Even when the criticism was true, I often reacted emotionally before I had time to process it.

Another thing that felt accurate was this:

> INFJs value honesty and authenticity, but they are also private. They often struggle to open up about their problems because they don't want to burden others.

Again, very relatable.

I have no problem listening to other people's problems, but sharing my own feels uncomfortable. As a result, I sometimes end up carrying things alone for much longer than I should.

## Parts That Felt Like a Mirror...

When reading about INFJs, the biggest thing wasn't the strengths. It was the weaknesses.

The descriptions talked about being sensitive to criticism, struggling with perfectionism, focusing too much on flaws, chasing ideal goals while neglecting small practical steps, and constantly putting other people's needs ahead of their own.

Reading those parts felt like looking into a mirror.

Not because I had never noticed these traits before, but because I had noticed them and simply ignored them for years.

For the first time, I had a framework that encouraged me to acknowledge those patterns and actively work on them.

That's probably the biggest value MBTI has given me.

Not certainty.

Not identity.

Not proof that I belong to some rare personality category.

Just a starting point for self-reflection.

## My Thoughts...

Whether MBTI is scientifically valid or not, I don't think that matters as much to me right now.

What matters is that it encouraged me to think about my strengths, my flaws, and the habits that might be holding me back. If a personality framework helps me become a little more self-aware, a little kinder to myself, and a little better toward others, then I think it has served a useful purpose.

And if nothing else, I'm happy to be on the same team as people and characters often associated with INFJs, such as Nelson Mandela, Martin Luther King Jr., Daredevil, Jon Snow, Obi-Wan Kenobi, Loki, and Dr. James Wilson.

Although I am slightly concerned that the list also includes people like Thanos, Ra's al Ghul, Itachi Uchiha, and Adolf Hitler.

So apparently the path from "wise mentor" to "world domination" is shorter than I expected.

Let's hope I stay on the correct side of that spectrum. Also, you should try this to...!!
